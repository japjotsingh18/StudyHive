# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in StudyHang welcoming and harassment-free for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or other protected characteristic.

We aim for a community where students, educators, designers, operators, and first-time open-source contributors can disagree constructively and learn safely.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and patient.
- Critique ideas and code, not people.
- Assume good intent while taking harmful impact seriously.
- Welcome questions and explain project-specific context.
- Respect privacy; never expose student identities, private messages, reports, locations, or security information.
- Accept moderation decisions and use the appeal path rather than escalating conflict publicly.
- Give credit and avoid plagiarism or misrepresentation.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, discrimination, or sexualized attention.
- Trolling, insults, personal attacks, or sustained disruption.
- Publishing or threatening to publish private information.
- Impersonation, academic misconduct facilitation, or abuse of university identity.
- Pressuring contributors or students to disclose personal circumstances.
- Retaliation against reporters, moderators, or reliability appeals.
- Any conduct that would be inappropriate in a professional or academic community.

## Scope

This code applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, community spaces, project events, and public situations where someone represents StudyHang.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not report conduct incidents in a public issue. Until a private conduct address is published, contact the repository owner through GitHub's private reporting/contact mechanisms. Maintainers must acknowledge reports promptly, protect reporter privacy, avoid conflicts of interest, and document proportionate outcomes.

Possible responses include a private correction, warning, temporary participation restriction, removal from project spaces, or permanent ban. Severe safety or legal threats may be escalated to the relevant platform or authority.

People responsible for enforcement must recuse themselves when they are involved in the report. Good-faith reports and appeals must not lead to retaliation.

## Attribution

This policy is inspired by the Contributor Covenant and adapted for StudyHang's student-safety and privacy context. Before the first public community launch, maintainers should publish a dedicated private conduct contact and a full enforcement procedure.
